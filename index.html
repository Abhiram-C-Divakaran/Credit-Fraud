Evidence-Based Fraud Model Learning Lab

Explore empirical decision-threshold trade-offs, concept drift, and temporal stability evaluated on 284,807 real-world credit card transactions (492 frauds, 0.173% prevalence) recorded across European cardholders over 48 hours in September 2013. The models utilize 28 anonymized PCA features (V1–V28) plus Time and Amount.

⚠️
Strict Model & Data Separation: The browser demo evaluates a 20-tree classifier on 6 synthetic behavioral inputs. This Learning Lab inspects offline scikit-learn models (50-tree Random Forest ensemble and Logistic Regression baseline) evaluated on historical PCA components. Benchmark metrics describe offline historical behavior and do not predict client-side demo outcomes. This benchmark is for educational analysis and does not establish production readiness.

💡 Operational Threshold Economics

Every threshold selection represents an explicit financial and customer experience compromise:

  • Fraud Interception Benefit: Catching 57 frauds avoids direct chargeback losses and interchange reimbursement liabilities.
  • Customer Friction Cost: 16 false positives require manual review or customer verification, generating operational friction.
  • Missed Fraud Liability: 18 missed frauds slip past detection and lead to financial recovery costs.
Tuning Principle:

In high-imbalance fraud environments (0.173% prevalence), threshold selection must be guided by Precision-Recall curves and validation tuning rather than default 0.50 cutoffs or ROC-AUC.

Single-split evaluations can hide concept drift. Below are results across three sequential expanding chronological windows.

🌲 Random Forest Invariance

Random Forest maintained consistent discrimination across all 3 windows (PR-AUC 0.7727 to 0.8291, Mean: 0.8043 ± 0.0288). Recall remained steady between 71.9% and 77.9%, and precision stayed above 90.3% (peaking at 97.1% in Window 2).

📈 Concept Drift Vulnerability

While Logistic Regression maintained high recall (84.0% to 89.5%), its precision collapsed from 37.1% in Window 3 down to 3.9% in Window 1 (1,250 false alarms). Linear hyperplanes struggled with shifting fraud cluster density without constant threshold recalibration.

⚠️ Non-Independence Disclosure

Windows share expanding historical training horizons and sequential temporal autocorrelation over a single 48-hour recording period. Aggregate spread describes empirical trajectory across this timeline rather than independent identically distributed variance.
